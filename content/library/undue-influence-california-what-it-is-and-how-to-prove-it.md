What is undue influence? The short answer: excessive persuasion that overcomes a person's free will and produces an unfair result. That is the undue influence California statute in one sentence (Probate Code § 86; Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70). If a caregiver, a sibling, or a new spouse pushed your parent into signing a document they would never have signed alone, this is the claim, and it sits at the center of our [undue influence and capacity](/undue-influence-and-capacity/) work.

Here's the thing families get wrong. Undue influence is not a feeling. It is a set of facts you prove with records, and the law puts the burden on you (Probate Code § 8252). A frail parent, a helper who ran the house, a lawyer nobody had heard of, and a document that flipped a lifelong plan: that is a case. A parent who simply liked one child more: that is not.

## How does California law define undue influence?

Since 2014 California has used one definition in probate court and in elder abuse cases. Probate Code § 86 adopts the Welfare and Institutions Code definition. Undue influence "means excessive persuasion that causes another person to act or refrain from acting by overcoming that person's free will and results in inequity" (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70).

The statute lists four things a court "shall" consider. Score your own facts against each one:

| Factor               | What the statute counts                                                                                                           |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vulnerability        | Illness, disability, age, impaired thinking, emotional distress, isolation, or dependency, and whether the influencer knew        |
| Apparent authority   | A fiduciary, family member, care provider, doctor, lawyer, or spiritual adviser; an agent under a power of attorney fits squarely |
| Tactics              | The red flags in the next section                                                                                                 |
| Equity of the result | What it cost your parent, how far it strays from the earlier plan, and whether the change fits the relationship                   |

No single factor decides it; a judge weighs all four. The court in Lintz v. Lintz said the 2014 law "eliminates any doubt that the two standards are now the same" in probate and elder abuse cases. A will, or any part of one, "procured by duress, menace, fraud, or undue influence" is ineffective (Probate Code § 6104), and courts void trusts on the same ground.

## What are the red flags of undue influence?

Section 15610.70 spells out the tactics. Read the list against what happened in your family:

- **Control.** Controlling "necessaries of life, medication, the victim's interactions with others, access to information, or sleep" is the first one listed.
- **Affection, intimidation, or coercion.** Sudden devotion from a new friend, or threats to put a parent in a home.
- **Haste and secrecy.** Property changes made in a hurry, "at inappropriate times and places," by someone with "claims of expertise" (same section). A new lawyer, a hospital-room signing, and nobody told the family until after the funeral.

The classic undue influence trust amendment in Silicon Valley moves a Sunnyvale or Cupertino house to one person, weeks before death, through a lawyer the parent never used. Run that pattern through the four factors. A parent in hospice, dependent on one helper for food and care, is vulnerable, and a helper who holds the power of attorney has apparent authority. Calling in a new lawyer and keeping the family out are tactics the statute names. A plan that hands everything to a newcomer is the inequitable result (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70).

## What is the presumption of undue influence in California?

In an undue influence California contest, the court expects you to carry the burden. Two presumptions flip it.

The statutory presumption comes first. Probate Code § 21380 presumes a gift in a will or trust is "the product of fraud or undue influence" when it goes to the person who drafted it, a fiduciary who transcribed it, or a care custodian of a dependent adult. For the care custodian, the document must have been signed during the care or within 90 days of it. The recipient must disprove undue influence by clear and convincing evidence, and if they fail they pay everyone's attorney's fees (Probate Code § 21380). For the drafter, the presumption cannot be rebutted at all.

A care custodian is anyone providing health or social services to a dependent adult, down to "companionship, housekeeping, shopping, cooking, and assistance with finances" (Probate Code § 21362). In Bernard v. Foley the California Supreme Court held that unrelated friends who nursed a dying woman in their home were care custodians, friendship or not. The Legislature later exempted unpaid friends whose relationship predates the care by set periods (Probate Code § 21362), so the timeline matters.

The statutory presumption does not reach relatives within the fourth degree or someone who lived with your parent (Probate Code § 21382). A sibling who inherits everything is not presumed anything. That is the second presumption's job; see [what you can do when a sibling or caregiver got everything](/library/sibling-or-caregiver-got-everything-what-you-can-do/).

The common-law presumption is older and broader. The California Supreme Court restated it in Rice v. Clark. The burden shifts when you show three things: the influencer "had a confidential relationship with the testator"; the influencer "actively participated in procuring the instrument's preparation or execution"; and the influencer "would benefit unduly by the testamentary instrument" (Rice v. Clark). It works against anyone: a sibling, a second spouse, a new best friend. In plain terms, your parent trusted and depended on this person, this person got the document made, and the gift is out of line with the earlier plan and the family tree.

Once either presumption applies, the recipient must prove the document was not the product of undue influence. Under the statute, that proof must be clear and convincing (Probate Code § 21380). Without a presumption, the burden of proof for undue influence in California stays on you, and it is heavy. In Doolittle v. Exchange Bank the court repeated the rule: the contestant "bears the burden of proving undue influence" and must prove it by clear and convincing evidence.

## What is not undue influence under California law?

An unequal estate plan, by itself. The statute says so: "Evidence of an inequitable result, without more, is not sufficient to prove undue influence" (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70). A parent may leave more to the child who moved home and less to the one who calls at Christmas. The statutory presumption exempts relatives on purpose (Probate Code § 21382).

Lack of capacity. That is a different claim with a different test. A parent can understand exactly what they are signing and still sign because someone overcame their will. The will statute lists undue influence as its own ground, apart from capacity (Probate Code § 6104). The two claims usually travel together; see our guide to [lack of testamentary capacity in California](/library/lack-of-testamentary-capacity-california/).

## How do you prove undue influence in California?

Proof is built, not asserted. The claim is pleaded as a ground in a [trust contest](/library/how-to-contest-a-trust-in-california/) or a will contest, and the record is the same either way. There is no jury in probate court unless a statute provides one (Probate Code § 825). A judge in San Jose decides, and the judge reads records. Here is where the evidence lives in a Santa Clara County contest:

| Evidence                    | Where it lives and what it shows                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Medical records             | Charts from Stanford, Kaiser, El Camino, Good Samaritan, or Valley Medical near the signing date; vulnerability, and whether the influencer knew |
| The drafting lawyer's file  | Who called for the appointment, who paid, who sat in the room, whether the lawyer ever met your parent alone                                     |
| Bank and property records   | Statements, new joint accounts, deeds recorded with the Santa Clara County Recorder in the last months of life, power of attorney transactions   |
| The influencer's own words  | Texts, emails, voicemails, posts; people who isolate a parent usually document it                                                                |
| Witnesses and earlier plans | The regular doctor, the old lawyer, the friend who stopped being allowed to visit, and every prior will and trust                                |

The other side builds a record too. In Doolittle the mother had obtained six certificates of independent review before she died, and the court let the trust pay to defend the amendment. Expect the same.

## What should you do if you suspect undue influence?

1. **Preserve everything.** Do not clean out the house. Photograph medication bottles, calendars, and notes on the fridge. Save texts and voicemails.
2. **Request the trust in writing.** A beneficiary or heir who asks after a death is entitled to a full copy of the trust terms (Probate Code § 16061.5).
3. **Calendar the deadline.** If the trustee mailed you a formal notice, you have 120 days from the mailing date to file a trust contest (Probate Code § 16061.8). Read [how long you have to contest a trust or will](/library/how-long-do-i-have-to-contest-a-trust-or-will-in-california/).
4. **List the medical providers.** Every doctor, hospital, and hospice from the last two years, with dates.
5. **Call a lawyer this week.** Bring the new document, the old one, the trustee's letter, and anything in the influencer's own words.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is undue influence in plain English?

Someone used pressure or control to get a vulnerable person to sign something they would not have signed alone, and the result is unfair. The statute calls it "excessive persuasion" that overcomes free will and "results in inequity" (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70). Win the claim, and the will, trust, or deed is thrown out.

### Is undue influence a crime?

Not by itself. It is a civil wrong, and because taking an elder's property by undue influence is financial elder abuse (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.30), the elder abuse statutes add remedies. Theft, embezzlement, forgery, or fraud against a person 65 or older is a separate crime (Penal Code § 368) that the District Attorney prosecutes. A police report does not get the house back; the civil case does. See our guide to [financial elder abuse of a parent](/library/financial-elder-abuse-of-a-parent-california/).

### Can a spouse be guilty of undue influence?

Yes. Spouses owe each other "the highest good faith and fair dealing" (Family Code § 721). In Lintz v. Lintz a third wife had her husband move his separate property into community property and sign trusts that disinherited two of his three children. The Court of Appeal held that when one spouse gains an advantage from a transaction, a presumption of undue influence arises and that spouse must rebut it. The voided trusts stayed voided. If the spouse began as a paid caregiver, see [what California law allows when a caregiver married your parent](/library/caregiver-married-my-parent-what-california-law-allows/).

### Does a certificate of independent review end the case?

No. A certificate is an independent lawyer's signed statement that they counseled your parent away from the beneficiary and found no fraud or undue influence. It only removes the statutory presumption (Probate Code § 21384); you can still prove undue influence the ordinary way, and the certificate itself can be attacked. The statute sets the terms: the lawyer must have no relationship with the beneficiary and no trustee or executor role or payout under the document (Probate Code § 21370), and the counseling must happen out of the presence of any heir or beneficiary (Probate Code § 21384). A meeting with the beneficiary in the room does not meet them.

### What if my parent seemed fine?

They can be fine and still be a victim. Capacity asks whether your parent understood the document; undue influence asks whether the choice was really theirs. Vulnerability under the statute includes illness, isolation, emotional distress, and dependency; none requires dementia (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15610.70). The people who say a parent seemed fine are often the ones who were kept away.

### How long do I have to bring an undue influence claim?

For a trust, 120 days from the day the trustee mails the formal notice after death (Probate Code § 16061.8). For a will, object before it is admitted to probate or petition within 120 days after (Probate Code § 8270). Financial elder abuse runs four years from discovery (Welfare and Institutions Code § 15657.7), or one year from the influencer's death if they have died (Code of Civil Procedure § 366.2). Filing late is worse than not filing: in Meiri v. Shamtoubi a daughter who sued about 230 days after the notice lost her whole inheritance under a no-contest clause (Probate Code § 21311).
